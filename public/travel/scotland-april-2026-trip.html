April 18-28, 2026 • Edinburgh to the Highlands & Islands

Trip Overview

Experience the magic of Scotland on this 10-day self-drive journey from April 18th (morning arrival via overnight flight) through April 28th, 2026. From Edinburgh's medieval charm to the mystical Loch Ness, the ancient Isle of Lewis, and the dramatic landscapes of Skye, this springtime adventure covers the best of the Scottish Highlands.

Total Distance: Approximately 850 miles (1,370 km)

💡 Essential Tips for April Travel

  • Drive on the left side of the road
  • Book ferries to the islands in advance (Ullapool-Stornoway, Stornoway-Uig)
  • April weather can be unpredictable - pack layers and waterproof clothing
  • Daylight hours: approximately 6am-8:30pm in April
  • Book accommodations in advance, especially on Skye and Lewis
  • Fill up with petrol when you can - stations are sparse in the Highlands
  • Roads may still have ice/snow at higher elevations - check conditions
  • Consider midges (tiny biting insects) from May onwards - bring insect repellent

🥾 Optional Long Hike (2–4 Hours)

For those seeking a more immersive hiking experience, consider one of these classic routes:

  • Old Man of Storr Circuit (2–3 hours): Start from the main car park, ascend to the iconic pinnacle, then loop around the back of the rock formations before descending. Moderate difficulty with steep sections and uneven terrain. 🗺️ Trail Map & Details
  • Quiraing Full Loop (3–4 hours): Begin at the Quiraing car park, follow the main path past the Prison and Needle, continue to the Table plateau, and return via the ridge. Offers sweeping views of Staffin Bay and the mainland. 🗺️ Trail Map & Details

Gear & Conditions: Wear waterproof hiking boots and dress in layers—spring weather on Skye can be changeable, with wet and muddy terrain common. April daylight extends from around 6am to 8:30pm, giving ample time for a longer outing. You'll likely encounter few other tourists this time of year.

Flexibility: The long hike fits easily into the day's schedule. If you prefer a shorter walk, the standard 1–2 hour Old Man of Storr route or a brief Quiraing viewpoint stroll are excellent alternatives.

⭐ Don't Miss:

The Quiraing at sunrise or sunset is magical. In April, you'll likely have fewer tourists and dramatic cloud formations. Bring waterproofs!

🥾 Fairy Pools Hike

Distance: 2.4 km (1.5 miles) one way, 4.8 km (3 miles) round trip
Duration: 1.5–2.5 hours round trip (allow extra time for photos and exploration)
Difficulty: Easy to moderate—relatively flat path with rocky and uneven sections
Elevation Gain: Minimal (approximately 50 meters / 165 feet)
🗺️ Trail Map & Detailed Guide

Trail Description: The Fairy Pools are a series of crystal-clear, turquoise pools and waterfalls formed by the River Brittle as it flows down from the Black Cuillin mountains. The well-trodden path follows the river upstream through a dramatic glacial valley. Each pool offers stunning photo opportunities, especially with the rugged Cuillin peaks as a backdrop.

What to Expect in April:

  • Water Levels: Higher than summer due to spring melt, making the waterfalls particularly impressive
  • Crowds: Fewer visitors than peak summer months—arrive early morning for near-solitude
  • Water Temperature: Very cold (around 6-8°C / 43-46°F) — brave souls may attempt a dip, but it's not for the faint of heart!
  • Snow: Possible snow patches on the surrounding peaks, creating a beautiful contrast with the pools

Essential Gear:

  • Waterproof hiking boots (trail can be muddy and wet)
  • Waterproof jacket and trousers (weather changes quickly)
  • Warm layers (fleece or insulated jacket)
  • Camera or smartphone with good battery—you'll want plenty of photos!
  • Walking poles optional but helpful on uneven terrain

Getting There: From Portree, drive south towards Carbost, then follow signs to Glen Brittle. The car park for the Fairy Pools is clearly marked (about 45 minutes from Portree). Parking can fill up even in April on sunny days, so arrive before 10 AM if possible. Small parking fee applies.

Pro Tips: The first few pools are the most dramatic and photographed. Many visitors turn back after the lower pools, but continuing upstream reveals more secluded cascades. The path becomes rougher further up, so assess conditions and your comfort level. Early morning or late afternoon light is best for photography.

📅 Sunday Operations Note

This is a Sunday - Most outdoor attractions are always accessible, but please note:

  • Talisker Distillery: Tours may be limited or unavailable on Sundays. Check and book in advance, or consider visiting on another day.
  • Dunvegan Castle: Typically open on Sundays during April, but confirm opening hours when booking.
  • All natural sites (Fairy Pools, Neist Point, Elgol) are accessible any day.

⭐ Don't Miss:

The Fairy Pools are magical - crystal clear blue water cascading down the rocks. Early morning is best for photos and fewer visitors.

🏨 Accommodation:

Stay in Portree or nearby

  • Optional: Short walk in Glencoe (weather dependent)
  • Drive past Loch Lomond
  • Visit Loch Lomond Bird of Prey Centre (near Balloch) - flying displays and interactive bird experiences
  • Arrive in Edinburgh late afternoon/early evening

🦅 Loch Lomond Bird of Prey Centre

Location: Near Balloch, at the southern end of Loch Lomond (approximately 25 miles from Glasgow, on the route to Edinburgh)

What to Expect:

  • Flying Displays: Watch stunning flight demonstrations featuring eagles, hawks, owls, and falcons. Displays typically run 2-3 times daily.
  • Interactive Experiences: Opportunity to handle and photograph birds of prey with experienced handlers
  • Duration: Allow 1.5-2 hours for a full visit including a flying display
  • Best for: Wildlife enthusiasts, photographers, families

April Visit Tips:

  • Opening Hours: Typically open daily from 10:00 AM. Check website for specific April hours and display times.
  • Booking: Pre-booking is recommended, especially for weekends and school holidays
  • Weather: Outdoor displays may be affected by high winds or heavy rain. Indoor viewing areas available.
  • What to Bring: Camera with good zoom lens for photography. Dress warmly in layers as displays are outdoors.

Timing in Your Day: This stop fits well in the late afternoon on your journey from Glencoe to Edinburgh. The centre is conveniently located just off the A82/A811 route, adding only 15-20 minutes to your overall journey. Consider arriving around 2-3 PM to catch an afternoon flying display before continuing to Edinburgh.

⭐ Don't Miss:

Eilean Donan Castle is one of Scotland's most photographed castles - arrive early for the best photos! Glencoe valley is breathtakingly dramatic with its towering peaks. Stop at the Glencoe Visitor Centre for stunning views and walking trails.
